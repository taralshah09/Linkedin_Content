# “How Does X Work?” — 45-Day LinkedIn Content Calendar

**Series start:** 16 August 2026  
**Series end:** 29 September 2026  
**Cadence:** One post every day, including weekends  
**Audience:** People who enjoy clear, thoughtful technology content  
**Scope:** Real-world products only. Each topic is framed around the user-visible product experience and the major systems that could power it, without assuming access to proprietary implementation details.

## Editorial direction

The calendar progresses from familiar real-time experiences to increasingly complex marketplaces, media systems, communication products, and personalized platforms. The intention is to make every post explain one recognizable product behavior, one useful technical idea, and one set of architectural trade-offs.

For each post, the accompanying Excalidraw HLD can generally follow this visual grammar: **user surfaces on the left, API or edge layer next, core product services in the center, asynchronous/event-driven components below, and data stores or external providers on the right**. Add numbered arrows for the primary request flow and a separate color for background events, notifications, analytics, or machine-learning pipelines.

## Suggested weekly rhythm

The sequence intentionally uses a recurring rhythm rather than treating every day as an unrelated topic. Early-week posts focus on a familiar product flow, mid-week posts isolate a deeper subsystem, and weekend posts cover a more visual or surprising system such as media delivery, booking contention, or multiplayer synchronization. This makes the calendar easier to sustain while keeping the series varied.

## Topic-selection guardrails

These topics should be presented as **architectural explanations or plausible reference designs**, not claims about confidential internals of any named company. Where a product’s exact implementation is unknown, the post can explicitly say: “Here is one way this could be designed at scale.” That framing preserves technical honesty while still giving the reader a useful HLD.

The strongest diagrams will show one primary user journey rather than attempting to draw the entire company. For example, the Cricbuzz post should focus on one score event moving from source to viewer, while the Uber post should focus on one ride request moving from pickup selection to driver assignment. Cross-cutting concerns such as authentication, observability, analytics, and disaster recovery can be shown as small supporting blocks rather than dominating the diagram.
